## Hub-and-Spoke Content Model

### The Model
Build your X presence around a hub-and-spoke structure where hub content establishes deep authority and spoke content drives discovery.

### Hub Posts (1-2 per week)
- **What:** Long-form threads, deep analyses, comprehensive guides
- **Purpose:** Establish authority, create reference-worthy content, build your knowledge base
- **Characteristics:** 800-2000+ words, high bookmark rate, cited by others, evergreen
- **Examples:** "The Complete Guide to AI Agent Architectures", "I Tested 15 AI Coding Tools — Here's What I Found"

### Spoke Posts (daily, 3-5 per day)
- **What:** Replies, quote tweets, short observations, news commentary
- **Purpose:** Drive traffic to hub content, maintain visibility, build relationships
- **Characteristics:** 1-3 tweets, timely, reactive, conversation-starting
- **Examples:** Replying to a trending AI thread, commenting on news, sharing a data point

### The Connection
Every spoke should indirectly reinforce the hub's topic domain. If your hub is "AI tools for developers," your spokes should consistently relate to developer tools, even if they're about news, opinions, or replies.

### Anti-Pattern
- Posting only spokes (reactive, no authority)
- Posting only hubs (slow growth, no network effects)
- Hubs and spokes on completely unrelated topics

## E-E-A-T Framework (Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness)

Originally a Google search quality framework, now directly applicable to X's algorithm through Tweepcred.

### Experience
Share first-hand usage, experiments, and results. Don't just report on things — USE them.

**How to demonstrate:**
- "I tested this for 2 weeks — here's what happened"
- "In my workflow, this saved me 4 hours per day"
- "I built a prototype using [tool] — here's the code"
- Share screenshots of your actual usage, not marketing materials

### Expertise
Demonstrate deep domain knowledge that goes beyond surface-level observations.

**How to demonstrate:**
- Explain WHY something works, not just THAT it works
- Compare alternatives with specific benchmarks
- Predict outcomes based on technical understanding
- Use precise terminology correctly (not buzzword salad)

### Authoritativeness
Reference your track record and get cited by other credible accounts.

**How to demonstrate:**
- Reference your previous accurate predictions
- Cite your own data/research
- Get quoted or retweeted by high-Tweepcred accounts
- Build a consistent body of work on a specific topic

### Trustworthiness
Be transparent about uncertainty, correct your own mistakes, and never mislead.

**How to demonstrate:**
- When wrong, acknowledge it publicly and quickly
- Clearly distinguish between facts, opinions, and predictions
- Disclose conflicts of interest (if you're affiliated with a tool you're reviewing)
- Use precise language: "Based on the data I've seen" vs "Everyone knows"

## 3-Second Rule

Your first line has approximately 3 seconds to stop someone's scroll. This is not a metaphor — it's based on average mobile scroll speeds.

### What Works in 3 Seconds
1. **Specific number + unexpected context:** "$47M raised for a 2-person team"
2. **Name-drop + contrarian:** "Sam Altman is wrong about [X]"
3. **Time-stamped urgency:** "In the last 72 hours, 3 major shifts happened"
4. **Curiosity gap:** "The one metric OpenAI doesn't publish"
5. **Concrete claim + implication:** "GPT-5 processes 1M tokens. Here's what that breaks."
6. **Unexpected comparison:** "Claude 4's coding ability ≈ a mid-level engineer at $0.002/line"

### What Fails in 3 Seconds
1. "Thread 🧵" — overused, provides no information
2. "Hot take:" — overused, signals engagement bait
3. "Just in:" — generic, no specificity
4. "Unpopular opinion:" — often followed by a very popular opinion
5. "Interesting development in AI" — vague, no payload
6. "Things are changing fast" — no duh, no specifics

### Testing Your Hook
Read ONLY your first line. Ask yourself:
- Would I stop scrolling for this?
- Do I want to know more?
- Does it promise something specific?

If any answer is "no," rewrite the hook.

## Dark Social Strategy

84% of X content sharing happens via "dark social" — channels you can't track: DMs, screenshots, copy-paste to other apps, verbal mentions.

### Designing for Dark Social
Every post should pass the dark social test:
1. **Self-contained:** Makes complete sense without context, thread, or profile
2. **Quotable:** Has at least one line worth screenshotting
3. **Data-dense:** Contains a specific number or fact worth sharing
4. **Compact:** Fits in a screenshot without awkward cropping
5. **Shareable:** The person sharing it looks smart/informed for doing so

### The Screenshot Test
Before posting, ask: "Would someone screenshot this and send it to a friend or coworker?"

If yes → high dark social potential → more shares, more reach
If no → the post relies on algorithmic distribution alone (riskier)

### Format Optimization for Dark Social
- **Single data point posts** are most screenshot-worthy
- **List posts (3-5 items)** are commonly screenshotted
- **Before/after comparisons** perform well
- **"According to [Source]..."** frames are trusted and shared
- Avoid posts that require reading a thread to understand

## Pattern Interruption Techniques

Pattern interruption breaks the reader's automatic scroll behavior by presenting something unexpected in the context they're scrolling through.

### Technique 1: Specific Number + Unexpected Context
```
"$47M for a 2-person AI startup."
→ The number ($47M) creates curiosity, the context (2-person) creates surprise
```

### Technique 2: Name-Drop + Contrarian
```
"Dario Amodei is wrong about AI safety timelines."
→ Named authority + disagreement = immediate attention
```

### Technique 3: Time Pressure
```
"In the last 72 hours, 3 major AI shifts happened."
→ Time constraint + incomplete information = must-read
```

### Technique 4: Curiosity Gap
```
"The one metric OpenAI doesn't publish — and why it matters."
→ Promises specific information the reader doesn't have
```

### Technique 5: Unexpected Comparison
```
"GPT-5's medical diagnosis accuracy: 89.7%. Average doctor: 82.3%."
→ Comparison creates instant context and surprise
```

### Technique 6: Counter-Intuitive Claim
```
"The best AI coding tools are the ones that write LESS code."
→ Contradicts expectation, demands explanation
```

### Anti-Patterns (What Looks Like Pattern Interruption But Isn't)
- ALL CAPS SHOUTING — looks desperate, not intriguing
- Clickbait headlines — "You won't BELIEVE this" — instant scroll past
- Too many emojis — looks like spam
- Overly long first lines — if it wraps to 3 lines on mobile, it fails

## Content Pillars

Every post should align with at least one of these four pillars. Over a week, aim for a mix of all four.

### Pillar 1: News Breakdown (30-40% of posts)
- **What:** Timely analysis of AI developments
- **Format:** News + your specific angle or insight
- **Strength:** Timely, relevant, easy to produce
- **Weakness:** Short shelf-life, competitive (many others cover same news)
- **Best practices:** Be fast (within hours), add unique analysis, cite sources
- **Example:** "Anthropic's new safety paper shows Claude can self-correct 94% of harmful outputs. What this means for enterprise adoption..."

### Pillar 2: Tool Reviews (20-30% of posts)
- **What:** Hands-on testing with honest benchmarks
- **Format:** Experience-based review with specific data
- **Strength:** High credibility, high bookmark rate
- **Weakness:** Time-intensive, requires actual usage
- **Best practices:** Be honest (include cons), use specific metrics, compare to alternatives
- **Example:** "I tested Cursor vs Copilot for 2 weeks on real projects. Cursor won on speed (+40%), Copilot won on accuracy (+12%). Here's the breakdown..."

### Pillar 3: Opinion/Hot Take (15-20% of posts)
- **What:** Clear stance backed by evidence
- **Format:** Claim + evidence + implication
- **Strength:** High engagement potential, builds personal brand
- **Weakness:** Risky — can get ratioed, alienates some audience
- **Best practices:** Always have evidence, acknowledge the counterargument, be willing to be wrong
- **Example:** "Open-source AI will not win the enterprise market. Here's why: support, compliance, and integration matter more than model quality. Counter-argument:..."

### Pillar 4: Educational (10-20% of posts)
- **What:** Explainers that teach something non-obvious
- **Format:** Concept explanation + practical application
- **Strength:** High bookmark rate, evergreen, builds authority
- **Weakness:** Lower immediate engagement
- **Best practices:** Teach ONE thing per post, use analogies, make it practical
- **Example:** "Most people use RAG wrong. They stuff documents into a vector DB and call it a day. The real magic is in the retrieval strategy — here's a framework that works..."

## Audience Building Strategy

### Growth Phases

**Phase 1: 0-1,000 Followers (Foundation)**
- Focus: Reply strategy — engage with larger accounts' content
- Post: 1-2 original posts per week, 5-10 replies per day
- Goal: Get noticed by accounts with 10K+ followers
- Avoid: Starting with threads (no one reads threads from 0-follower accounts)

**Phase 2: 1,000-10,000 Followers (Growth)**
- Focus: Mix of original content and strategic replies
- Post: 3-5 original posts per week, 3-5 replies per day
- Goal: Find your top-performing content type and double down
- Key: Track what triggers Phoenix for your account

**Phase 3: 10,000-50,000 Followers (Authority)**
- Focus: Hub content (threads, deep analyses)
- Post: 1-2 threads per week, 2-3 short posts per day, selective replies
- Goal: Become a reference point in your niche
- Key: Build relationships with other authority accounts

**Phase 4: 50,000+ Followers (Influence)**
- Focus: Thought leadership, platform contributions
- Post: Quality over quantity, 1-3 high-impact posts per day
- Goal: Shape conversations in your niche
- Key: Your replies carry weight — use them strategically

### Universal Growth Tactics
1. **Consistency:** Post at least once per day. Inconsistency kills algorithm favor.
2. **Engagement reciprocity:** Accounts that engage with you get your content boosted by the algorithm.
3. **Profile optimization:** Bio should clearly state your expertise. Pinned tweet should be your best hub content.
4. **List building:** Being added to curated lists signals authority to the algorithm.
5. **Cross-platform:** Mention your X account on other platforms (LinkedIn, blog, newsletter).
